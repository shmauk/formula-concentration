Cheat sheet

Making up infant formula at a Target concentration

From an Australian or New Zealand formula label to a recipe of whole scoops and water to the nearest 5 mL.

Key terms

Concentration
Energy density of prepared formula, always in kcal per 30 mL.
Target volume / Target concentration
The bottle you want to make.
Reconstitution statement
The label's "powder + water makes this much" line, e.g. "1 scoop + 50 mL water makes approximately 55 mL".
kcal per scoop
Energy in one level scoop of powder.
Displacement
The volume (mL) one gram of powder adds beyond its water, in mL per g.
Recipe
A whole number of scoops plus water rounded to 5 mL.
Actual volume / Actual concentration
What the recipe really makes, after rounding.

Part A · Reading the label

Find: energy per 100 mL of prepared formula, the Reconstitution statement, and grams per scoop.

  1. A1 Scoops in the statement = grams of powder ÷ grams per scoop (skip if it counts scoops)
  2. A2 Displacement = (prepared volume − water) ÷ grams of powder (3 dp; scoops × grams per scoop if it counts scoops). Usually 0.55–0.90 mL per g however big the scoop; if not, re-read the label.
  3. A3 kcal per scoop = energy per 100 mL × (prepared volume ÷ scoops) ÷ 100 (1 dp)

Part B · Building the recipe

  1. 1 kcal/mL = Target concentration ÷ 30
  2. 2 kcal needed = Target volume × kcal/mL
  3. 3 Scoops = kcal needed ÷ kcal per scoop, rounded up to a whole scoop
  4. 4 Actual kcal = scoops × kcal per scoop
  5. 5 Total powder = scoops × grams per scoop
  6. 6 Total displacement = total powder × displacement
  7. 7 Water = Actual kcal ÷ kcal/mL − total displacement, rounded up to the nearest 5 mL
  8. 8 Actual volume = water + total displacement
  9. 9 Actual concentration = Actual kcal ÷ Actual volume × 30

Check: if the Actual concentration is 0.5 kcal/30 mL or more below the Target concentration, double-check your inputs and working before using the recipe.

Rounding

  • Scoops: always round up, unless the answer is already exactly whole.
  • Water: round up to a 5 mL mark, the smallest amount you can measure reliably on a bottle, unless it's already on one.
  • Show kcal, grams and volumes to 1 dp, displacement to 3 dp, Concentration to 2 dp. Keep the unrounded numbers in your calculator between steps.
  • So the recipe always comes out at or just below the Target concentration, and at or just above the Target volume.

Worked example: NAN OPTIPRO 1, 150 mL at 24 kcal/30 mL

NAN OPTIPRO 1: the label says

  • Energy: 67 kcal per 100 mL
  • Reconstitution statement: 129 g powder + 900 mL water makes 1 L
  • Grams per scoop: Average scoop weight = 4.3 g

Part A · Reading the label

StepWorkingAnswer
A1 Scoops in the statement 129 ÷ 4.3 30 scoops
A2 Displacement (1000 − 900) ÷ 129 0.775 mL per g
A3 kcal per scoop 67 × (1000 ÷ 30) ÷ 100 22.3 kcal

Part B · Building the recipe

StepWorkingAnswer
1 kcal/mL 24 ÷ 30 0.8
2 kcal needed 150 × 0.8 120 kcal
3 Scoops 120 ÷ 22.3 = 5.38, round up 6 scoops
4 Actual kcal 6 × 22.3 133.8 kcal
5 Total powder 6 × 4.3 25.8 g
6 Total displacement 25.8 × 0.775 20.0 mL
7 Water 133.8 ÷ 0.8 − 20.0 = 147.3, round up to 5 mL 150 mL
8 Actual volume 150 + 20.0 170.0 mL
9 Actual concentration 133.8 ÷ 170.0 × 30 23.61 kcal/30 mL

Recipe: 6 scoops + 150 mL water

Makes: 170.0 mL at 23.61 kcal/30 mL

0.5 kcal/30 mL or more under target? No

Common mistakes

  • Making the water equal to the Target volume. The powder takes up room too: subtract the total displacement.
  • Rounding scoops to the nearest whole scoop instead of up.
  • Forgetting to round the water up to a 5 mL mark.
  • Dividing by scoops instead of grams of powder when working out displacement.
  • Dividing by grams per scoop when the Reconstitution statement already counts scoops.
  • Mixing up per 100 mL (on the label) with per 30 mL (Concentration).
  • Leaving out the check in step 9 when the bottle is small and the scoop is large.
